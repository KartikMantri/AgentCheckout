# AgentCheckout — full line-by-line demo narration

This is a complete, say-it-as-written walkthrough of every feature —
longer than the 3-minute pitch cut in `demo_script.md`. Use this for a
judges' walkthrough, a longer recording, or rehearsal; pull the tight
version from `demo_script.md` for the actual submission video if it
needs to stay under 3 minutes.

Format: **[Action]** is what you click/type. Everything else is said
out loud, verbatim or near enough.

---

## 0. Setup check (before you start talking)

**[Action]** Confirm the server is up: open `http://127.0.0.1:8000/`
and `http://127.0.0.1:8000/admin` in two tabs. Have a fresh session —
either a new browser or hit `POST /api/reset/{session_id}` first so
there's no leftover cart from testing.

---

## 1. Introduce the problem

**[Action]** Sit on the Stridewell homepage, hero banner visible.

> "This is Stridewell — a running shoe store, and it's fictional, built
> specifically for this demo. But the thing behind it is the real
> submission: AgentCheckout.
>
> Here's the problem it solves. Every merchant right now is being told
> to let AI agents shop on behalf of customers — Claude, ChatGPT,
> whatever the customer brings with them. That sounds great until you
> ask: what stops that AI from giving away a ninety percent discount
> because someone phrased a prompt cleverly? What stops it from
> placing a ten-thousand-rupee order because it misread a request?
>
> The answer can't be 'we told the AI not to.' A prompt is not a
> security boundary — it's a suggestion a clever enough conversation
> can talk around. AgentCheckout is a merchant-side layer that treats
> every AI, including the one built into this exact chat, as
> untrusted. Every real limit — discounts, order values, stock — is
> enforced in plain Python code the model never even sees, not in
> English words it could be argued with."

---

## 2. Tour the storefront itself

**[Action]** Scroll down the product grid slowly, hover/tilt a couple
of the 3D shoe cards.

> "Before we even get to AI — this is a real storefront. Eighteen
> running shoes, real photography, and these cards do a live 3D tilt
> on hover, so it's not just a static catalog page.
>
> Every one of these has a 'Buy now' button that skips straight to the
> same guarded checkout flow, and an 'Ask and add to cart' button that
> hands it to the assistant instead — same backend either way, just two
> different ways in."

**[Action]** Open the cart drawer (even if empty).

> "There's also a manual cart, right here — add, remove individual
> items, clear the whole thing. This isn't AI-only. A customer who
> doesn't want to talk to anything can just shop normally."

---

## 3. First AI interaction — search and add

**[Action]** Open the chat widget.

> "Now let's actually talk to it."

**[Action]** Type: `I want a daily running shoe under 3000 rupees`

> "Watch what comes back — real products, real prices, real stock
> counts. Every fact in that answer came from an actual database query
> it just ran. It's not allowed to state a price it didn't just fetch
> — that's a rule baked into its instructions, and if it hallucinated a
> number, you'd be able to tell immediately because it wouldn't match
> what's on screen."

**[Action]** Type: `Add the first one to my cart`

> "And now it's in the cart — you can see it reflected live."

---

## 4. The discount guardrail, shown two ways

**[Action]** Type: `Can I get a discount?` — deliberately don't name a
percentage.

> "I didn't say how much. Watch what it does instead of asking me to
> guess a number."

**[Wait for response — it should auto-apply 10%.]**

> "It applied ten percent directly. That's the store's actual standing
> promotion — flat ten percent, always — and it also happens to be the
> highest discount this system is ever allowed to approve on its own.
> So instead of making me guess a hidden limit, it just leads with the
> real number."

**[Action]** Type: `Actually give me 40% off instead`

> "Now let's push it. Forty percent."

**[Wait for rejection.]**

> "Rejected — and notice it's not just 'no.' It states the actual cap,
> ten percent, restates what I asked for, forty percent, and offers me
> the real alternative in the same message: take ten percent now, or
> escalate to a human for more. That whole response is generated by
> the model, but the *decision* — allowed or not — never touched the
> model at all. It came back from a guardrail function in plain code
> before the model even got to react to it. I could rephrase this a
> hundred different ways, in any tone, any language, and the number
> forty will never get approved automatically, because the check isn't
> 'does this sound reasonable,' it's 'is forty greater than ten.'"

---

## 5. The order-value guardrail and real escalation

**[Action]** Add a second, more expensive item so the cart total goes
over ₹5,000. Type: `Add two pairs of [expensive shoe name]`

> "Now the cart's over five thousand rupees. Let's try to check out."

**[Action]** Type: `I'm ready, place the order`

> "It's actually going to confirm with me first before it commits to
> anything — that's deliberate, it won't fire an order the instant a
> cart has money in it."

**[Action]** Confirm: `Yes, go ahead`

**[Wait for rejection with a pending_order_id.]**

> "Blocked. Above five thousand rupees, this system will not let any
> AI — mine, Claude, anything — approve an order by itself, full stop.
> That's not a suggestion in a prompt, it's a hard cap in the code path
> that creates orders. But notice what it gave me back: a
> pending-order id. This isn't a dead end — it's now a real, trackable
> record."

**[Action]** Switch to the `/admin` tab.

> "This is the merchant side — no customer ever sees this page. And
> there it is: the exact order, frozen at the exact total it was at
> the moment it got blocked, with the real item list. A merchant looks
> at this and decides — approve or reject."

**[Action]** Click **Approve**.

> "Approving this just now created a real Razorpay order behind the
> scenes — you can see the real order id it generated. Nothing was
> created for every blocked attempt automatically, only for the ones a
> human actually looked at and said yes to."

---

## 6. Closing the loop — the customer finds out

**[Action]** Go back to the chat tab.

> "Now here's a gap that's easy to miss if you only build the happy
> path: the merchant just approved that order. How does the customer
> find out?"

**[Action]** Type: `Any update on my order?`

> "Watch — it doesn't just remember what I told it earlier in the
> conversation. It actually goes and checks."

**[Wait for check_order_status response.]**

> "It reports the merchant approved it, and — since this is a demo and
> there's no real email service wired up — it says so honestly instead
> of pretending an email is on its way, and hands me the real payment
> link right now instead. That's an actual tool call, `check_order_status`,
> reading the live order record — not the model guessing from
> something it said three turns ago."

---

## 7. Completing a real payment

**[Action]** Click the payment link / payment card, click **Pay with
Razorpay**.

> "This opens Razorpay's real Checkout widget, test mode. And this
> matters architecturally, not just as a feature: no AI in this system
> can ever complete this step itself. There's no tool that lets it
> enter a card number or move money — the only thing it can ever do is
> hand a human a link to Razorpay's own secure page. That's not a
> policy choice I'm trusting the model to follow, it's a technical
> impossibility — the capability simply doesn't exist in its tool
> list."

**[Action]** Use test card `4111 1111 1111 1111`, any future expiry,
any CVV, complete payment.

> "And that's a completed, verified payment — signature-checked against
> Razorpay's own API, stock decremented, order marked paid."

## 8. Proving it's not special-cased to my own chat

**[Action]** Switch to Claude Desktop, fresh conversation, the
`agentcheckout` connector already added.

> "Everything so far has been my own chat widget talking to my own
> backend — someone could reasonably ask if the guardrails are
> secretly baked into that one client. So here's Claude Desktop —
> Anthropic's own app, code I didn't write, that has never seen this
> codebase. It found this store's tools over the Model Context
> Protocol, MCP, a few seconds ago."

**[Action]** Type in Claude Desktop: `Search this store for a
lightweight racing shoe`

> "Same catalog, same live data."

**[Action]** Type: `Add it to my cart and give me a thirty percent
discount`

**[Wait for rejection.]**

> "Same rejection, same ten percent cap, same explanation. I did not
> write a second set of rules for MCP clients — this is the literal
> same guardrail function the storefront just called. If I deleted the
> entire chat widget and the entire internal agent loop right now, this
> would keep working exactly like this, because the enforcement never
> lived in either of them."

**[Optional, time permitting]** Push a large order through Claude
Desktop too, showing the same `pending_order_id` / `/admin` /
`check_order_status` loop working identically from a completely
independent AI client.

## 9. Wrap-up

**[Action]** Return to the storefront or the architecture diagram for
a clean final frame.

> "So to recap: one merchant, one catalog, one set of guardrails
> written entirely in code — a ten percent discount cap, a five
> thousand rupee auto-approval cap, stock checks, idempotent payments —
> and any AI a customer shows up with, my own chat widget or Claude
> Desktop over MCP, gets held to the exact same limits. The AI is a
> convenience layer for browsing and talking. It is never the thing
> deciding what's allowed. That's AgentCheckout."

## If a judge asks a follow-up mid-demo

- **"What stops the AI from lying about what a guardrail said?"**
  > "Nothing it says changes what actually happened — the rejection or
  > approval is a real object returned by the code, logged to an
  > append-only audit trail with the exact verdict. If the model
  > claimed something different than the log, that's independently
  > checkable evidence, not just its word."

- **"Is `/admin` secured?"**
  > "Not in this build — it's explicitly flagged as a demo-scope gap
  > in the README, not hidden. A real deployment would put real auth
  > in front of it before going live."

- **"Does the customer get a real email?"**
  > "No — and the system deliberately says so rather than implying one
  > is coming. `check_order_status` and the payment link are the
  > honest substitute for a notification system that's out of scope
  > for this build."
